# VisionFX Color Distribution Research

## Sources reviewed

The design research combines the anti-AI-slop UI color budget with practical color-allocation guidance from [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/how-the-60-30-10-rule-saved-the-day-934e1ee3fdd8), [Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/types-of-color-palettes/), and [WP Mayor](https://wpmayor.com/the-60-30-10-rule-made-our-website-designs-infinitely-better/). These sources treat the 60–30–10 rule as a starting framework rather than a universal law: the dominant color establishes the page’s emotional baseline, the secondary color structures the page, and the accent is reserved for attention and action.

For VisionFX, the previous page overused a very pale canvas as both the dominant and secondary role. This made the page feel washed out rather than editorial. The revised system will use a **deep ink/near-black dominant surface at roughly 50–55% of the visible homepage**, **warm sand and parchment structural surfaces at roughly 35–40%**, and **traffic orange under 6%**. The remaining visual weight comes from neutral artwork and borders. This preserves orange as a voltage signal while ensuring the site has stronger value contrast and an unmistakable dark foundation.

The implementation will use dark surfaces for the header, hero, services, and footer; sand for project and studio context; and parchment only as a restrained reading or card surface. The primary CTA band will remain orange, but the orange field is the one deliberate conversion moment rather than a repeated decorative device. Text colors and focus states will be tested against both dark and light surfaces.

## Design decision

> VisionFX should feel **ink-led, not paper-led**. The page begins and ends in near-black, uses sand as its structural counterweight, and lets parchment function as a local reading surface rather than the default page background.
